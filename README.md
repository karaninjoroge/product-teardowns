---

## Why Teardowns?

Product Managers live in two worlds simultaneously: the product they are building and the competitive landscape around it. Teardowns train the analytical muscle of product thinking — the ability to look at an existing product and quickly identify what is working, what is broken, who it is built for, and what you would do next.

A recruiter reading a teardown can immediately tell whether a candidate thinks like a PM or like a user. Users notice what they like and dislike. PMs ask why a decision was made, what constraint drove it, and what opportunity it reveals.

## Teardown Standards

- Every claim about a user problem must be grounded in observed behavior, not assumption
- Opportunities section must be specific and prioritized — not a wishlist
- KPIs section must define what you would measure as PM, not generic metrics
- Minimum 800 words per teardown — depth over breadth
